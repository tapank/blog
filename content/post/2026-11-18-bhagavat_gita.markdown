When I was a teenager, my grandfather advised me to make a habit of reading the
Bhagavad Gita regularly. I am finally at a point where I now read a chapter
every week.

But I had not imagined it would take me so long to get to this point. I was led
astray, for decades, by available options in the bookstores. Over time, I
bought the first two of the list below, and every time I tried to read, I
failed to get past the first 50 to 100 pages. These books, though well
intentioned, distract the reader from the fact that the Bhagavad Gita itself is
very accessible. All you need is a verse-by-verse translation in a language of
your choice. Each verse is very deep but it does not require a multi-page
commentary found in these books listed below. These books seem to go to great
lengths to demonstrate the "depth of knowledge" of the author, as if they were
asked to write a thesis, and the most complicated and convoluted and lengthy
submission wins.

- Bhagavad-gita As It Is (Swami Prabhupada): ~980 – 1,008 pages
- Gita Rahasya (Lokmanya Bal Gangadhar Tilak): ~1,000 – 1,050 pages
- The Holy Geeta (Swami Chinmayananda): ~1,130 – 1,270 pages
- God Talks with Arjuna (Paramahansa Yogananda): ~1,224 pages
- Sadhak Sanjivani (Swami Ramsukhdas): ~1,200 – 1,280 pages
- Essays on the Gita (Sri Aurobindo): ~576 – 610 pages

A typical Indian wanting to study the Gita is very likely to be put on the path
that I tried to navigate, and when they fail at making any progress in
understanding the Gita, they may incorrectly assume that the text is beyond
them -- they are not yet ready to grasp it. This is unfortunate because they
were never guided to the fact that Gita is way more accessible than what they
have been led to believe. Of course, every time you read Gita, you walk away
with new insights that make you fall in love with the text even more.

If I had known, I would have started with a simple translation at the very
beginning, instead of struggling with the huge volumes which were very
difficult to read.

I found the following edition from Gita Press the most accessible of all. It is
just a Hindi translation, and no more. It is possible to read the whole book
cover to cover in a few hours. A chapter can be read in 15 - 20 minutes. The
typeface is set to a very large size, perhaps keeping aging readers in mind. If
this were in a normal-sized typeface, the book would be a third of its current
size. I actually like the large typeface.

- Shrimad Bhagavad Gita (Shri Jayadayal Goyandka): 256 pages

If you have ever wanted to read the Bhagavad Gita and are lost because you are
wrestling with one of the voluminous texts, may I suggest that you read a
translation (without commentary). You may be pleasantly surprised by how
accessible it is.
